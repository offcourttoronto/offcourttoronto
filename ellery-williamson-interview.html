Team Canada youth athlete and Cornell volleyball player Ellery Williamson on pressure, mental resets, school, and building a high-performance environment that still feels joyful.

In her own words

Hi Offcourt fans! My name is Ellery Williamson. I’m from Toronto, Ontario, and I’m an outside hitter. I played club at Phoenix Volleyball Club on the Legacy 18U team.

I won two consecutive club national championships; first at 17U in 2024, then at 18U in 2025; and received All-Canadian honours in 2023, 2024, and 2025. In the summer of 2024, I represented Canada as a member of the youth national team at the FIVB U17 World Championships in Lima, Peru. My next chapter is Division I volleyball at Cornell University.

Ellery’s responses were shared with Offcourt earlier in 2026 and have been lightly edited for clarity.

The conversation

01

When did playing stop feeling like “just fun” and start feeling like high performance?

I started playing club in my 14U year. When I moved to Legacy, I was 15 and playing up on a 17U team. At the time, it felt like a big jump. I was playing with older girls at a higher level and competing against tougher teams.

Having supportive coaches and teammates by my side made all the difference. Together, we were able to have fun and cultivate a high-performance environment at the same time, which made practice my favourite part of the day.

02

What did a normal week look like while balancing school, training, and Team Canada commitments?

In high school, I would practise three times a week, spend time in the gym, and play tournaments on the weekend. As a student-athlete, it is very important to communicate with the teachers and staff at your school about your time commitments and work together to create a plan that keeps you on track in the classroom.

You’ll obviously have busier weeks than others, but building strong study habits for yourself will make the stressful times feel a lot more manageable.

03

Was there a moment when the mental side of sport felt harder than the physical side?

I was grateful to play in a handful of championship matches, both provincial and national, during my years at Phoenix. In big games, when there is a lot on the line, it can be scary to be the one to take a big swing or serve aggressively. In those moments, you have to lean on your teammates and trust all the hours of practice you have put in.

It is the worst feeling when you lose a game knowing you made the safe choice and regretting that you had not gone for it. Even though it can be mentally hard in the moment, trust yourself and stay aggressive on the court; no matter the score or the stakes.

04

Did you ever feel like athletes are “supposed” to be tough?

It can be really hard to brush off mistakes. Especially in volleyball, where almost every point ends in an error, being able to mentally reset when you are struggling is so important.

Something that helped me change my mindset when one part of my game was lacking was putting my energy into another skill instead of shutting down completely. If I was making attacking errors, for example, I would focus on my serve-receive or defence. This helped me reset my game while still contributing positively to my team.

05

What would you tell a younger athlete dealing with the same pressure?

The pressure is never going to go away, so embrace it! Club is such a special experience that goes by so fast. Enjoy every practice, travel day, and tournament you have with your teammates. There is nothing like it.

Ellery’s reminder

“Trust yourself and stay aggressive on the court; no matter the score or the stakes.”

Offcourt takeaway

A reset can be
a redirection.

A reset does not always mean instantly forgetting a mistake. Sometimes it means directing your energy toward the next way you can help: the next serve-receive, defensive play, encouraging word, or controllable action.

Ellery’s experience also shows that high performance and joy do not have to cancel each other out. Supportive teammates, honest communication, and strong habits can make both possible.
